# 05 — School Forms (SF1–SF10) Strategy

*Covers Part 19 of the audit brief — SF1–SF10 strategy and gap analysis.*

---

## The governing principle

> **SF1–SF10 are outputs of the academic information system. They are not the system.**

This is the most important architectural statement in the whole set, and it is easy to get wrong. The tempting design is to build tables that mirror the forms. That design produces a product that can print SF9 and nothing else, breaks the moment DepEd revises a layout, and needs re-engineering for every school with a variant.

The correct design is three layers:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  LAYER 1 — CORE ACADEMIC DATA                                │
│  students · enrollments · classes · assessments · scores ·   │
│  grades · attendance · personnel                             │
│  Knows nothing about any form.                               │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                            ↓
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  LAYER 2 — REPORT DATA SOURCES                               │
│  A named, versioned contract per form. A SQL view or         │
│  function returning a stable JSON payload.                   │
│  e.g. rds.report_card_v1(enrollment_id, period_id)           │
│  Knows what a form needs; knows nothing about its layout.    │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                            ↓
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  LAYER 3 — TEMPLATE BINDINGS  (per school, versioned)        │
│  HTML/CSS template + field map + page setup + signatories    │
│  Knows layout; knows nothing about the database.             │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                            ↓
              PDF · Excel · print-ready HTML
```

**What each layer boundary buys:**

- A school with a slightly different SF9 gets **a new template**, not a code change.
- DepEd revising a layout means **updating templates**, not migrating data.
- A brand-new form a school invents is **a new data source + template**, and the core model is untouched.
- The same data source feeds a PDF, an Excel export, and an on-screen view without duplication.

**What must never happen:** a form template reaching into tables directly, or a core table gaining a column that exists only because a form has a box for it.

## PART 8 — SF1–SF10 Analysis

### Confidence markers

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Confirmed** | Verifiable from public DepEd issuances |
| **Likely** | Standard practice; high confidence, unverified for any specific school |
| **School-specific** | Genuinely varies; must be configuration |
| **Requires validation** | Assumption made to keep moving; confirm before deployment |

> **Overall status: we do not hold any school's official SF templates.** Form *purposes* below are Confirmed from public DepEd issuances. Exact **field lists, column orders, page layouts, and print dimensions are Requires-validation** and must be taken from the school's actual files during onboarding. This is by design — the three-layer architecture means that gap delays *template authoring*, not *platform development*.

### What this tells us about scope

**Fully derivable from the core academic model:** SF1, SF2, SF4, SF5, SF6, SF9, SF10. These seven are the platform's business, and every one of them is a *rendering* problem once the data model in [06 Data Architecture](06-data-architecture.md) exists.

**Needs data the platform does not otherwise hold:**

| Form | Missing input | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| SF3 | Textbook inventory | **Out of scope.** A property module solves a different problem and dilutes the product. If a school insists, it is custom work. |
| SF7 | Personnel profiles (position, employment status, qualifications) | **Phase 2, low cost.** Mostly a matter of extending the user record; the teaching-load half already exists in `class_assignments`. |
| SF8 | Health measurements | **Phase 3 at earliest.** Genuinely a different domain, and health data raises its own privacy considerations. |

**Deliberate position:** Mendtrix should say plainly that the platform covers academic records and their forms, and does not cover property or health. A product that claims all ten forms and does three of them badly is worse than one that does seven of them properly.

### Data-origin classification

Per the brief, each field on each form must be classified by where its value comes from. This drives the template binding model.

| Origin | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Database — direct** | Read from a column | Learner name, LRN, section, date of birth |
| **Database — computed** | Derived server-side by the grading or attendance engine | Period grade, general average, days present, promotion status |
| **Configuration** | From school settings, not academic data | School name, school ID, region, division, logo, signatory names |
| **Workflow-derived** | A product of the approval process | Date finalized, approving registrar, issuance number, issue date |
| **Manual entry at generation time** | Genuinely cannot be derived; typed when the document is produced | Adviser's narrative remarks, observed-values ratings, special annotations |

> **Design rule.** *Manual entry* is a last resort and every instance of it is a re-entry cost. Where a field recurs (adviser remarks per learner per period), it must be **captured once into the database** — as a field in the record book — and thereafter read as *Database — direct*. The number of manual-entry fields at generation time should trend to near zero as the product matures; tracking that number is a useful measure of whether the product is actually eliminating duplicate work.

### Template binding model

Each form template is a versioned, school-owned artifact:

```yaml
template:
  id:            sf9_report_card
  school_id:     <tenant>
  version:       3
  data_source:   rds.report_card_v1        # the Layer 2 contract
  effective_from: 2026-06-08
  page:
    size:        A4
    orientation: portrait
    margins:     { top: 10mm, right: 10mm, bottom: 10mm, left: 10mm }
  assets:
    logo:        school.logo_url
    letterhead:  school.letterhead_url
  bindings:
    learner_name:      $.student.display_name
    lrn:               $.student.lrn
    section:           $.enrollment.section_name
    grades_table:      $.subjects[*]
    general_average:   $.summary.general_average
    days_present:      $.attendance.present_days
    adviser_remarks:   $.remarks.adviser          # captured in the record book
  signatories:
    - { role: adviser,   name: $.staff.adviser_name,   label: "Class Adviser" }
    - { role: principal, name: $.config.principal_name, label: "School Principal" }
  numbering:
    enabled: false      # report cards are typically unnumbered
```

**Versioning is mandatory, not optional.** A report card reprinted in 2029 for the 2026 school year must render with the 2026 template and the 2026 signatories — not today's. The template version and the signatory set in force at issuance are recorded on the generated document.

### Working without the official templates

We do not have ANHS's SF files, and the brief is explicit that this is not a blocker. Concretely:

**What can be built now, with no template in hand:**

- The entire core academic model (Layer 1)
- Every Layer 2 data source, since these are driven by what the *data* means, not what a form looks like
- The template engine, binding syntax, numbering, versioning, and PDF pipeline
- The document issuance log and archival
- Generic report card and promotion report templates built from public DepEd layouts, good enough for a pilot

**What genuinely waits for the school:**

- Exact field lists, column orders, and page dimensions for each form
- The observed-values / behaviour rubric used on the report card
- Signatory titles, order, and any specimen signatures
- Whether the school uses a customised variant of any form
- Any school-invented form not in the SF series

**How the gap is closed:** it is step 3 of onboarding — *collect the school's current Excel files and blank forms* — see [15 Onboarding & Discovery](15-onboarding-and-discovery.md). Template authoring is a 1–3 day implementation task per school once the files are in hand, and it is billable implementation work, not product development.

> This separation is precisely the point of the three-layer architecture. **Not having the templates delays template authoring by days. It does not delay the platform by a single day.**

### Traps to avoid

Recording these explicitly because each is a plausible mistake that would be expensive to unwind:

1. **Do not create an `sf9_data` table.** The moment a table is named after a form, the form has become the model.
2. **Do not add a core column because a form has a box.** Ask what the value *means* academically. If it means nothing outside the form, it belongs in the template layer as manual entry — or the form field is asking for something the school should be capturing properly elsewhere.
3. **Do not hard-code "three terms" into report-card templates.** The subject-grades table must iterate over however many periods the school year declares. This is the same trap V0 fell into throughout.
4. **Do not assume SF numbering is stable across DepEd issuances.** Forms have been renumbered and revised before. Bind templates to a *data source contract*, never to a form number.
5. **Do not let one school's variant become the default.** When the second school needs a different SF9, resist editing the shared template. Create a second template. If three schools share a variant, *then* promote it to a shared default.

## What the gap analysis shows

**V0 implements 6 of 10 forms — but as renderers over data it does not fully hold.**

Three patterns recur across the gaps:

1. **Fields rendered but never captured.** SF1 draws LRN, date of birth, age, and barangay columns and leaves them blank, because no screen collects them. The form exists; the data does not.
2. **Missing infrastructure, not missing form code.** SF2 and SF4 need a school calendar and learner-movement tracking. Both are absent, which is why V0's attendance denominator is wrong rather than merely incomplete.
3. **Consolidation by file exchange.** SF5 needs grades across all subjects. V0's only mechanism is a teacher exporting JSON and an adviser importing it (`main.js:1327`, `main.js:860`) — the exact manual step the product exists to remove.

**SF10 is the clearest illustration of why the rebuild is structural rather than cosmetic.** It requires a learner's record across multiple years and multiple schools. V0 keys grades by uppercased name and owns students by class. No amount of form work produces SF10 from that model — it needs the enrollment architecture in [06 Data Architecture](06-data-architecture.md) first.

## Build priority rationale

| Priority | Forms | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **MVP** | SF9 (report card), SF5 (promotion) | Without a printable report card the school keeps a parallel spreadsheet and the MVP fails its own test. SF5 closes the school year. |
| **Phase 2** | SF1, SF2, SF4, SF6, SF7, SF10 | Real obligations, but the pilot survives a term without them. ⚠️ **SF2/SF4 must land before the school's first monthly filing deadline after go-live** — a schedule commitment, not an aspiration. |
| **Phase 3** | SF8 | Different domain; needs its own privacy analysis. |
| **Out of scope** | SF3 | Property management. Recommend declining rather than doing it badly. |

> Mendtrix should state the coverage plainly in sales conversations: **seven of the ten forms come directly from this data; SF3 and SF8 belong to different systems.** A product that claims all ten and delivers three properly is worse than one that is honest about its boundary — and registrars respect the distinction, because they know which forms come from where.
